# ADR 0038 — The 8085 is not a z80, and pricing it correctly makes things worse

Status: **Rejected** (2026-09-15) as a code change. The **measurements stand**
and are recorded here, because they are correct and were expensive to get.

## The defect

`g0_word_cost` and `g0_word_bytes` both select a per-CPU row for KC160, EZ80,
VM1, RABBIT and GBZ80, and let everything else fall through to the z80 row.
**8085 falls through, and it is not a z80.** It has LDSI (`ld de,sp+N`) and
LHLX/SHLX (`ld hl,(de)` / `ld (de),hl`), all 10 cycles per `src/ticks`, which
reach a word slot in 20 cycles where the z80 walk
(`ld hl,n; add hl,sp; ld a,(hl); inc hl; ld h,(hl); ld l,a`) costs 44.

The catch is register splatter: LDSI lands the address in DE, so the sequence
wraps in `push de`/`pop de` whenever DE is live. Across the corpus 8085 output
that is **894 of 1836 word reads (49 %)** but only **4 of 804 writes**.

## The measured rows

Weighted by the mix the lowerer actually emits — for reads 894 wrapped / 942
bare LDSI / 390 z80-form, for writes 4 / 800 / 366:

| | read | write | deref | step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **cycles**, charged (z80 row) | 45 | 41 | 52 | 92 |
| **cycles**, measured 8085 | **33** | **26** | **44** | **65** |
| **bytes**, charged (z80 row) | 8 | 7 | 9 | 16 |
| **bytes**, measured 8085 | **5** | **4** | 9 | **10** |

A related figure, for whoever works on the lowerer: on 8085
`ex de,hl; ld de,sp+d; ex de,hl` puts a slot address in HL in **18 cycles / 4
bytes**, against 20 / 4 for `ld hl,nn; add hl,sp` — and if DE is dead,
`ld de,sp+d; ex de,hl` does it in **14 / 3**. The lowerer emits the z80 form at
2150 sites on 8085.

## The trap this walked into first

The step was priced at 36 on the theory that LDSI holds the address in DE across
a read-modify-write. It does not: **all 804 `ld (de),hl` sites sit exactly one
line after their own `ld de,sp+N`** — the address is re-formed for each half.

That wrong step measured *better* (−104 bytes) and ran *worse* — `lexbench`
**+4.3 % ticks while shrinking**. Which is the exact symptom, on the exact
bench, that the Z80 row's own comment records from the last time someone priced
a step by assuming address reuse. Read that comment before touching a STEP cost.

## Why it was rejected

With the step corrected, the accurate 8085 rows measure:

* **size +44 bytes**, 6 cells larger, **0 smaller**
* **ticks +0.037 %**, 4 cells slower, 2 faster
* no leak: every non-8085 cell of 720 byte-identical, z80 reference 422/422

So a demonstrably more accurate cost model produces demonstrably worse code.

The mechanism is visible in the numbers. A home's modelled benefit is
essentially `slot_cost − register_cost` per access. Charged, that is 45 − 8 =
37; measured, 33 − 8 = **25**, a 32 % cut. Every register home on 8085 becomes
worth a third less, the allocator opens fewer of them, and the output degrades.
Empirically, an 8085 register home is worth *more* than its per-access delta —
which is the same conclusion ADR 0037 reached from the other direction: the
binding quantity is not the price of an access.

There is a second-order reason to expect exactly this here. The 49 % wrap rate
is not a constant of the chip — DE is live under register *pressure*, and giving
a value a register home changes that pressure. Pricing the average wrap rate as
a fixed per-access cost bakes in the pressure the allocation is supposed to
relieve.

## Not a bug, though it looks like one

8085 has no index register, so the `(ix+d)` fp sub-row in both tables is fiction
for it. This is **latent**: 8085 fp and sp output are byte-identical in every
bench, with and without the correction, so nothing observable depends on it.
Worth fixing when the tables are next touched; not worth a commit on its own.

## What to do with this

Nothing, until the missing term from ADR 0037 exists. When a home's benefit
accounts for what it *causes* rather than what each access *costs*, come back,
paste these rows in, and re-measure — they are right, and this file exists so
nobody has to measure them again.
